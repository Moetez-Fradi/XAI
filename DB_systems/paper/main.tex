\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Modern vector databases return opaque scalar similarities over high-dimensional
embeddings, leaving practitioners without coordinate-level explanations and
without a way to \emph{route} HNSW search using only a dynamic focus subspace.
We present \textbf{XQdrant}, an extension of the Qdrant engine with two
complementary capabilities.
First, \texttt{with\_dims\_explained} elevates exact metric score decomposition
to a first-class, in-database post-search primitive, avoiding client-side vector
egress; on synthetic encoder-width corpora this yields about $2.2\times$ lower
attribution latency than post-query extraction at matched Recall@10.
Second, we study \emph{masked-distance} HNSW traversal via
\texttt{focus.masked}, a hybrid layer cutoff (\texttt{mask\_from\_layer}), SIMD
gather/repack kernels, an optional verify/overfetch pass, and a weighted score
blend (\texttt{alpha}).
Across SIFT1M and high-dimensional synthetic indices we find that hybrid cutoffs
recover navigability (SIFT recall $\sim$0.8--0.97 for cutoffs $\ge 1$) that
naive all-layer masking collapses, that gather kernels win microbenchmarks
($\sim$1.1--1.8$\times$) but do not unlock end-to-end speedups vs.\ full search,
that subspace$\leftrightarrow$full $k$-NN coherence predicts when verification
can help, and that plain verify at $\mathit{limit}{=}k$ cannot change Recall@$k$
whereas overfetch+verify recovers full-space recall on coherent data (SIFT) at
substantial latency cost.
A weighted blend is dominated by a hard layer cutoff.
We release an experiment index and reproducible harness so these negative and
positive results can be cited independently.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:}
vector databases, HNSW, approximate nearest neighbor, explainability,
feature attribution, subspace search, masked distance, SIMD
\end{abstract}

%=============================================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

High-dimensional dense embeddings underpin retrieval-augmented generation
(RAG), recommendation, and semantic search~\cite{lewis2020rag,qdrant2024,wang2021milvus}.
Production engines typically traverse HNSW graphs~\cite{malkov2018hnsw},
collapsing each comparison into a single scalar.
That design creates two gaps.
\emph{Explainability:} practitioners cannot see which coordinates drove a hit
without fetching full vectors and recomputing per-dimension terms outside the
database.
\emph{Subspace routing:} many workloads care about a dynamic focus set
$\mathcal{F}$ of coordinates (concept probes, ablations, modality masks), yet
stock HNSW always evaluates full $D$-dimensional distances during graph descent.
Preselect-and-rescore on $\mathcal{F}$ improves semantic specificity but cannot
reduce traversal work~\cite{xqdrant2026}.

\textbf{XQdrant} addresses both gaps inside a Qdrant-compatible Rust engine.
For attribution, \texttt{with\_dims\_explained} attaches exact top-$m$ coordinate
terms after search.
For subspace routing, \texttt{nearest.focus} with \texttt{masked=true} evaluates
reduced distances during HNSW, optionally with a hybrid layer cutoff
(\texttt{mask\_from\_layer}), gather-based SIMD kernels, a verify/overfetch
rescoring mode, and an $\alpha$-blend of full and masked scores.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{In-database attribution.} Opt-in exact score decomposition with
    order-statistic top-$m$ selection; recall-neutral vs.\ vanilla Qdrant and
    $\sim$2.2$\times$ faster than post-query extraction at encoder widths.
  \item \textbf{Masked-distance HNSW.} Layer-aware dual scorers, API-stable
    focus fields (\texttt{masked}, \texttt{mask\_from\_layer}, \texttt{verify},
    \texttt{alpha}), and gather/repack kernels selectable at runtime.
  \item \textbf{Empirical map of what works.} Hybrid cutoffs restore navigability
    on SIFT; kernels win microbenches but not wall-clock search; coherence
    explains high-$D$ plateaus; verify needs overfetch; $\alpha$-blend is a
    negative ablation vs.\ a hard cutoff.
  \item \textbf{Reproducible artifacts.} Scripts under \texttt{DB\_systems/research/}
    and a curated experiment index listing canonical CSVs/plots for each claim.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{HNSW and Contiguous SIMD}

HNSW maintains multi-layer proximity graphs~\cite{malkov2018hnsw}.
Queries greedily descend with candidate heap size \texttt{ef\_search}, then
extract top-$k$ at the base layer.
Distance kernels assume contiguous \texttt{f32} slices (AVX2/NEON), so a
non-contiguous focus set either (i)~repacks selected coordinates into a scratch
buffer or (ii)~gathers them in-register/\emph{via} gather loads.

\subsection{Score Decomposition}

For Dot product, $s(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{v})=\sum_i q_i v_i$; Cosine applies the
same after $\ell_2$ normalization; Euclidean/Manhattan admit analogous additive
terms~\cite{lundberg2017shap,ribeiro2016lime}.
Attribution is thus exact accounting of the engine's own metric---not a surrogate
XAI model.

\subsection{Subspace Coherence}

Let $N_k^{\mathrm{full}}(q)$ and $N_k^{\mathcal{F}}(q)$ be exact $k$-NN sets
under the full metric and under $\mathcal{F}$.
\emph{Coherence} $|N_k^{\mathrm{full}}\cap N_k^{\mathcal{F}}|/k$ predicts when
masked routing can surface full-space neighbors and when a cheap rescore can
help.

\section{In-Database Attribution}
\label{sec:attribution}

XQdrant injects attribution at the collection coordinator after shard merge,
with an early return when \texttt{with\_dims\_explained} is absent.
Per explained hit, Algorithm~\ref{alg:attribution} materializes $D$ terms and
selects the top-$m$ by absolute contribution in average $\mathcal{O}(D)$ time
(\texttt{select\_nth\_unstable}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\begin{minipage}{0.92\linewidth}
\small
\textbf{Algorithm 1} (In-database top-$m$ attribution).\\
\textbf{Input:} query $\mathbf{q}$, vector $\mathbf{v}$, $D$, $m$, metric $\delta$.\\
1.\ Normalize operands if required by $\delta$.\\
2.\ For $i=0,\ldots,D-1$: append $(i,\textsc{Term}_i(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{v},\delta))$.\\
3.\ Return order-statistic top-$m$ by $|t|$, sorted descending.
\end{minipage}
\caption{In-database top-$m$ feature attribution.}
\label{alg:attribution}
\end{figure}

\texttt{nearest.focus} (without masking) still performs full HNSW preselection
then rescoring on $\mathcal{F}$---useful for interpretability-constrained ranking
but not a traversal acceleration (Section~\ref{sec:eval-attr}).

\section{Masked-Distance HNSW Design}
\label{sec:masked}

\subsection{API Surface}

Focus parameters ride inside the existing query body:
\begin{verbatim}
"focus": {
  "dims": [...], "masked": true,
  "mask_from_layer": 1,   // optional hybrid cutoff
  "verify": true,         // optional full rescore
  "alpha": 0.25           // optional blend on focus layers
}
\end{verbatim}
Constraints mirror dense local search: no quantization/turbo, no simultaneous
\texttt{with\_dims\_explained} on masked paths, fail-fast if
\texttt{mask\_from\_layer}/\texttt{verify}/\texttt{alpha} are set without
\texttt{masked=true}.

\subsection{Option 1: All-Layer Masking}

With \texttt{masked=true} and cutoff omitted, every HNSW comparison uses only
coordinates in $\mathcal{F}$.
This is the cheapest semantic change but destroys navigability when the focus
metric disagrees with the index geometry.

\subsection{Option 2: Hybrid Layer Cutoff}

Layer $\ell$ uses the masked scorer iff $\ell < L$ for cutoff $L{=}\texttt{mask\_from\_layer}$;
otherwise full distance.
$L{=}0$ routes to plain nearest (no masking); a large $L$ recovers Option~1.
Implementation builds dual raw scorers inside \texttt{FilteredScorer} and switches
with \texttt{set\_layer} at layer boundaries---visited-set and heap logic remain
unchanged.

\subsection{Kernels: Repack vs.\ Gather (X1)}

Masked scoring historically copied focus dims into a scratch buffer then called
contiguous SIMD (\emph{repack}).
We add an in-place \emph{gather} path (AVX2 \texttt{vgatherdps} for Dot/Cosine/Euclid on
x86; scalar elsewhere), selected by \texttt{XQDRANT\_MASKED\_KERNEL}.

\subsection{Verify and Overfetch (X3)}

\texttt{verify=true} re-scores returned candidates with pure full distance after
traversal (same hook as quantization rescore).
At $\mathit{limit}{=}k$ this only reorders a size-$k$ set, so Recall@$k$ is
unchanged.
Overfetch ($\mathit{limit}{=}m{\cdot}k$, keep top-$k$) turns masked search into a
candidate filter.

\subsection{Weighted Blend (Option 4)}

On focus layers, $\textit{score}=\alpha\cdot s_{\mathrm{full}}+(1-\alpha)\cdot s_{\mathrm{focus}}$.
Coarse hybrid layers stay full-only.
Blend alone never reduces full-distance work; it only biases routing.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Setup}

\paragraph{Attribution suite.}
Synthetic corpora $N{=}10\mathrm{k}$, $Q{=}500$, $D\in\{768,1536\}$, seed~42;
baselines: vanilla Qdrant, post-query extract+client top-$m$, and XQdrant.
Hardware for those runs: Apple Silicon laptop (suite orchestrator).

\paragraph{Masked-distance suite.}
HTTP against a release XQdrant binary, typically with
\texttt{XQDRANT\_MASKED\_KERNEL=gather}.
Corpora: SIFT1M ($D{=}128$) and synthetic $N{=}50\mathrm{k}$ at $D\in\{768,1536\}$,
$Q{=}500$, $k{=}10$, $\texttt{ef\_search}{=}128$.
Canonical folders are listed in \texttt{DB\_systems/experiments/README.md};
figures below are copied into \texttt{paper/figures/}.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Vector databases.}
Milvus~\cite{wang2021milvus}, Pinecone~\cite{pinecone2023}, and
Qdrant~\cite{qdrant2024} expose ANN with filters and quantization but not native
per-coordinate decompositions or layer-aware masked metrics.

\paragraph{ANN libraries.}
FAISS~\cite{johnson2019faiss} and ScaNN~\cite{guo2020scann} optimize large-scale
search; explainability and dynamic subspace routing remain post-processing.

\paragraph{Explainable AI.}
SHAP/LIME~\cite{lundberg2017shap,ribeiro2016lime} approximate model behavior.
We instead exploit additive metric structure for deterministic attributions aligned
with ranking, closer to OLAP rollups~\cite{gray1998data}.

\paragraph{Dimension reduction / projections.}
Static projections or multi-index layouts differ from \emph{query-time} focus sets
over an existing full-dim HNSW.
Projected auxiliary indexes (Option~3) remain future work when focus palettes are
small and known a~priori.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What to ship.}
In-database attribution and hybrid \texttt{mask\_from\_layer} are the positive
systems results.
Gather kernels are worth keeping as an implementation detail.
Verify is useful as an optional correctness mode \emph{with} overfetch on coherent
data; $\alpha$-blend should not be marketed as improving Option~2.

\paragraph{Why no e2e speedup.}
Even with faster masked compares, HNSW still touches full vectors for storage,
graph structure, and (on hybrid coarse layers) full SIMD.
I/O and candidate management dominate the microbenchmarked arithmetic.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
High-$D$ Option~1 lacks a dedicated archive folder (high-$D$ collapse is evidenced
via Option~2/X2).
We did not instrument visited-set divergence (X4) or build projected indexes
(Option~3).
Attribution suite hardware differs from the Linux masked-suite host; absolute
milliseconds are not cross-compared across suites.

%=============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

XQdrant shows that exact feature attribution can live inside a Rust vector engine
with negligible quality loss, and that masked-distance HNSW is a viable
\emph{navigability} tool when coarse layers keep full distance---but not yet a
latency win, and not a substitute for coherent subspaces when the goal is
full-space recall.
Overfetch+verify and $\alpha$-blending clarify the design space: the former can
recover recall when candidates are in range; the latter is dominated by a hard
layer cutoff.
These measured positives and negatives, backed by a public experiment index, form
a concrete basis for subspace-aware vector search in production engines.
